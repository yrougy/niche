A diesel engine is a type of internal combustion engine that generates power through **compression ignition** rather than using spark plugs. While it shares the same four-stroke cycle as a gasoline engine, the process differs in several key ways:

### 🔹 The Four-Stroke Cycle in a Diesel Engine
1. **Intake**: Only air is drawn into the cylinder. Unlike gasoline engines, no fuel is mixed with the air at this stage.
2. **Compression**: The piston moves upward, compressing the air to a very high ratio (typically 14:1 to 25:1, compared to 8:1–12:1 in gasoline engines). This compression drastically increases the air's temperature to roughly 500–700°C (900–1,300°F).
3. **Power (Combustion)**: Near the top of the compression stroke, diesel fuel is injected directly into the hot, high-pressure air through precision fuel injectors. The fuel spontaneously ignites due to the heat and pressure, causing a rapid expansion of gases that forces the piston downward, producing mechanical work.
4. **Exhaust**: The piston moves upward again, pushing the burned exhaust gases out through the exhaust valve. The cycle then repeats.

### 🔑 Key Differences from Gasoline Engines
- **No spark plug**: Ignition occurs solely from compressed, superheated air.
- **Air-only intake**: Fuel is injected directly into the cylinder just before combustion.
- **Higher compression ratio**: Enables spontaneous ignition and greater thermal efficiency.
- **Throttle-less operation** (in many designs): Power is controlled by varying fuel injection, not by restricting airflow.

### ⚙️ Why Diesel Engines Are Efficient
- Diesel fuel has a higher energy density and ignites reliably under compression, allowing precise control over combustion timing.
- Engines run with excess air ("lean burn"), improving combustion completeness and reducing pumping losses.
- The high compression ratio extracts more mechanical work from each combustion event, yielding better fuel economy and high torque at low RPMs.

### 📌 Modern Enhancements
Today's diesel engines typically include:
- **Turbochargers** to force more air into the cylinder
- **Common rail direct injection** for precise fuel metering
- **Emission controls** like Diesel Particulate Filters (DPF) and Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) to meet environmental standards

In short, a diesel engine works by compressing air to extreme pressures and temperatures, then injecting fuel that ignites spontaneously, converting chemical energy into mechanical motion through repeated, highly efficient four-stroke cycles. This makes diesel ideal for heavy-duty applications like trucks, ships, generators, and construction equipment.
