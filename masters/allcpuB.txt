A diesel engine is an internal combustion engine that ignites fuel through **compression** rather than a spark plug. It operates on the same basic four-stroke cycle as a gasoline engine, but with key differences in how air and fuel are handled and how ignition occurs.

### 🔹 The Four-Stroke Cycle (Typical Diesel Engine)
1. **Intake**: The piston moves downward, opening the intake valve and drawing in **only air** (no fuel is mixed in at this stage).
2. **Compression**: The piston moves upward, compressing the air to a much higher ratio than in gasoline engines (typically **14:1 to 25:1**). This compression raises the air temperature to **500–700°C (900–1300°F)**, well above diesel's auto-ignition temperature (~210°C / 410°F).
3. **Power/Ignition**: Near the top of the compression stroke, diesel fuel is injected directly into the hot, high-pressure air by precision fuel injectors. The fuel instantly **auto-ignites** without a spark plug, causing rapid combustion that forces the piston downward, producing power.
4. **Exhaust**: The piston moves upward again, pushing the burned gases out through the exhaust valve.

### 🔑 Key Differences from Gasoline Engines
- **No spark plug**: Ignition relies solely on high compression heating the air.
- **Air-only intake**: Fuel is injected only during the compression/power stroke.
- **Throttle control**: Power is regulated by **fuel quantity**, not air flow (diesel engines don't use a traditional throttle plate).
- **Higher compression ratio**: Extracts more mechanical work from each combustion event.
- **Lean combustion**: Runs with excess air, improving thermal efficiency and reducing heat loss.

### ⚙️ Modern Enhancements
- **Turbocharging**: Forces more air into the cylinder, boosting power and efficiency.
- **Common-rail fuel injection**: Uses ultra-high-pressure pumps and electronically controlled injectors for precise fuel metering and multiple injections per stroke (reducing noise and emissions).
- **Emissions controls**: Diesel Particulate Filters (DPF), Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) with urea, and exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) are standard to meet environmental regulations.

### ✅ Why Diesels Are Efficient & Torque-Rich
- Diesel fuel has a higher energy density than gasoline.
- High compression and lean burn yield **30–40% better thermal efficiency** than gasoline engines.
- Combustion occurs over a longer duration, producing strong low-end torque ideal for heavy loads, towing, and continuous operation.

Diesel engines are widely used in trucks, buses, ships, construction equipment, and backup generators due to their durability, fuel economy, and longevity. Modern variants also include advanced electronics and emissions systems to balance performance with environmental standards.
